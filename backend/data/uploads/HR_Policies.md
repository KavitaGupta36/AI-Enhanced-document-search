## HR-001: Annual Leave and Absence Policy

**Document ID:** HR-001
**Version:** 3.0
**Effective date:** 1 January 2026
**Owner:** Human Resources
**Supersedes:** HR-001 v2.1 (effective 1 January 2024)
**Applies to:** All permanent employees of XYZ Company

### 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how employees earn, request, and take annual leave, sick leave, parental leave, and bereavement leave. It replaces all earlier versions of this policy.

### 2. Annual leave entitlement

2.1 Full-time employees receive 25 working days of paid annual leave per calendar year.

2.2 Leave accrues at 2.08 days per completed month of service. New joiners accrue leave from their start date.

2.3 Part-time employees receive annual leave pro-rated to their contracted hours.

2.4 Employees may carry over up to 5 unused days into the following calendar year. Carried-over days must be used by 31 March of that year or they expire.

### 3. Requesting annual leave

3.1 Requests for leave of more than 5 consecutive working days must be submitted through the HR Portal at least 10 business days before the first day of leave.

3.2 Requests for shorter periods should be submitted at least 3 business days in advance.

3.3 All requests require approval from the line manager. Managers should respond within 3 business days.

3.4 Leave may be refused where it would leave the team without the minimum cover set out in the team's Resourcing Plan. In that case the manager must give the reason in writing.

### 4. Public holidays

Employees receive 10 public holidays per calendar year, following the official calendar for the country in which they are employed. Public holidays falling on a weekend are not substituted unless local law requires it.

### 5. Sick leave

5.1 Employees receive 10 days of paid sick leave per calendar year.

5.2 Employees must notify their manager by 9:30 local time on the first day of absence.

5.3 A medical certificate is required for any absence longer than 3 consecutive working days.

5.4 Unused sick leave does not carry over and is not paid out on leaving the company.

### 6. Parental leave

6.1 Primary caregivers receive up to 16 weeks of parental leave paid at 100% of salary.

6.2 Secondary caregivers receive 4 weeks of paid leave paid at 100% of salary.

6.3 Employees must have completed 6 months of service to qualify. Adoption and surrogacy are covered on the same basis.

### 7. Bereavement leave

Employees receive 5 working days of paid leave on the death of an immediate family member, and 2 working days for other close relatives. Additional unpaid time may be agreed with the manager.

### 8. Leave on termination

Accrued but unused annual leave is paid out on termination, up to a maximum of 25 days. Carried-over days are included in this calculation.

### 9. Contacts

Questions about this policy should be directed to the HR Service Desk at hr-help@example.com.

## HR-002: Remote and Hybrid Work Policy

**Document ID:** HR-002
**Version:** 2.0
**Effective date:** 1 March 2026
**Owner:** People Operations
**Applies to:** All employees, excluding contractors (contractors follow their own agreements)

### 1. Overview

XYZ Company supports flexible working. Employees may work from an office, from home, or in a combination of both. Each team sets its own working pattern within the rules below.

### 2. Office hubs and hybrid attendance

2.1 The company operates three office hubs: London, Austin, and Bangalore.

2.2 Employees whose contracted base is a hub must attend the office at least 2 days per week, averaged over each calendar quarter.

2.3 Core collaboration days are Tuesday and Wednesday. Teams should schedule in-person meetings, workshops, and planning sessions on these days where practical.

2.4 Employees based outside a hub are classified as "remote-based" and are not required to attend an office. They may attend on request and the company will reimburse reasonable travel to a hub for planned team events.

### 3. Core working hours

All employees must be available for collaboration between 10:00 and 15:00 in their local time zone, unless they have an approved flexible working arrangement.

### 4. Working from another country

4.1 Employees may work from a country other than their employment country for up to 30 days in any calendar year without prior approval.

4.2 Any period beyond 30 days requires written approval from Global Mobility and a tax assessment. Working abroad without approval may create tax and immigration liability for the employee and the company.

4.3 Working from a sanctioned country is prohibited at all times. See COM-001 and the Sanctions Screening Procedure for the current list.

### 5. Equipment and home office support

5.1 Employees receive a one-time home office stipend of $500 for furniture, monitors, and ergonomic equipment. Claims are made through the standard expense process (see FIN-001).

5.2 The company provides a laptop and one peripheral kit. Personal devices may not be used to access company systems except through the approved mobile device management (MDM) enrolment.

### 6. Security requirements for remote work

6.1 Company-managed devices must be used for all work involving company data.

6.2 A company VPN must be used when connected to any public or untrusted network, including cafés, airports, and hotels.

6.3 Employees must lock their screen when leaving their workstation, including at home.

### 7. Exceptions

Requests for exceptions to this policy are made to the employee's People Business Partner and approved by the Head of Function and People Operations. Exceptions are reviewed every 12 months.

## HR-003: Learning and Development Policy

**Document ID:** HR-003
**Version:** 1.2
**Effective date:** 1 April 2025
**Owner:** Talent and Learning
**Applies to:** All permanent employees after completing their probation period

### 1. Purpose

XYZ Company invests in the growth of its people. This policy describes the learning budget, the support available for professional certifications, and the internal mentoring programme.

### 2. Annual learning budget

2.1 Each eligible employee receives an annual learning budget of $1,500 per calendar year. Unused budget does not carry over.

2.2 Eligible spend includes courses, conferences, books, and subscriptions to recognised learning platforms.

2.3 Any single course or item costing more than $300 requires pre-approval from the employee's manager before purchase.

2.4 Learning spend is claimed through the expense process described in FIN-001.

### 3. Professional certifications

3.1 The company will pay examination and registration fees for approved professional certifications, up to $400 per certification.

3.2 The certification must be relevant to the employee's current role or a role they are preparing for, as agreed with their manager.

3.3 Employees must remain with the company for 12 months after the certification fee is paid. If they leave earlier, the fee must be repaid on a pro-rated basis: for each full month short of 12 months, one-twelfth of the fee is repayable.

3.4 Certification study time of up to 2 hours per week may be scheduled during working hours with manager agreement.

### 4. Internal mentoring programme

4.1 Employees may request a mentor from another team at any level. Mentoring pairs meet at least once a month for a minimum of six months.

4.2 Participation is voluntary. Mentors do not have line-management responsibility for their mentees.

4.3 Requests are made through the Talent portal. Matching typically takes 4 to 6 weeks.

### 5. Performance and development reviews

Learning goals agreed during the annual performance review should be reflected in the employee's individual development plan. Managers are expected to review progress at each quarterly check-in.
